Collab Skill Brief: INFLUENCE
Working title: Influence Campaign Planner Authors: Greg Weinger & Ryan Latta (joint) Status: Draft for discussion — fill in the open questions, then we turn it into a SKILL.md Last updated: [date]


The one-line pitch
A guided workflow that helps an introverted technical leader plan and run an influence campaign for an initiative — mapping who matters, figuring out what each person responds to, sequencing the outreach, and drafting the actual messages — so the work that doesn't come naturally gets done anyway.
Why this one
Of all the skills in the set, this is the one where our two angles genuinely combine. Greg's lens is the introverted leader who knows the right move and skips it. Ryan's working material — the per-person notes in his vault, the "what's the right way to do the nudge?" question, the VP who "loves to talk about product" so the outreach leads with product — is basically the engine for this. We're not inventing a framework; we're encoding a habit Ryan already runs by hand.
Who it's for
Engineering managers, product managers, and ICs who want influence without formal authority — people who can do the thinking but avoid the relationship-building and the campaigning. The skill should assume the user is competent and busy. Its job is to reduce activation energy and produce artifacts, not to lecture.
When it should trigger
The user is trying to get an initiative adopted, get buy-in for a decision, change a team's direction, or "get people on board" with something. Phrases like: "I need to get people on board with X," "how do I get buy-in," "I want to pitch this to leadership," "nobody's paying attention to this proposal." (We can make the description a little pushy so it triggers even when the user doesn't say "influence.")
The workflow (draft — this is the heart of it)
Define the win. What's the initiative, what does "adopted" actually look like, what's the deadline or forcing function?
Map the landscape. Who decides, who can block, who's a swing vote, who's an ally already? (Power/interest grid as the underlying lens.)
Read each person. For each stakeholder: what do they care about, what currency do they respond to, what's the taboo? This is where Ryan's vault-notes pattern lives — the skill should ask for (or read) the user's read on each person and tailor accordingly.
Pick the frame. Offer a couple of named influence lenses (see below) so the user can choose how to think about it.
Build the story. One simple narrative: the value, told through a concrete example, not abstraction.
Sequence the outreach. Warm the 1:1s first, then back it with email/Slack, then group settings and department leads. The skill decides the right-sized move per person — email vs. quick touch-base vs. coffee.
Draft the messages. Produce the actual first-pass emails/Slack notes for the highest-priority people.
Influence models to potentially reference
Cohen & Bradford, currencies of influence (Influence Without Authority) — almost tailor-made for the no-authority IC.
Cialdini's principles (reciprocity, social proof, etc.) — useful as a checklist, used honestly.
Stakeholder power/interest grid — for the mapping step.
RACI / decision rights — to clarify who actually decides vs. who just has opinions.

Open question for Ryan: which of these do you actually use, and what's missing? You've got the consulting experience taht I don't.
Inputs and outputs
Inputs: the initiative, the list of stakeholders, the user's notes/read on each (free-form is fine — mirror Ryan's vault style).
Outputs: a stakeholder map, a sequenced outreach plan with the right-sized move per person, and drafted first-pass messages for the top few.
Division of labor (proposal)
Greg: overall structure, the introvert-specific framing, the message-drafting step, packaging it as the downloadable episode artifact.
Ryan: the stakeholder-read / per-person-notes mechanics, which influence models actually earn their keep, the "right way to do the nudge" decision logic.
Both: the workflow sequence and the test cases (a few realistic scenarios we run it against to see if the output is any good).
Open questions to settle before drafting the SKILL.md
Does the skill read structured notes (e.g., an Obsidian-style per-person file) or just ask the user inline? (Ryan's setup suggests reading; Greg's audience may not have a vault — maybe support both.)
How opinionated should the sequencing be? Prescriptive playbook vs. options menu?
Which influence model is the default frame, and do we let the user swap it?
What's the single demo scenario we'll show on the episode?




Ryan’s Thoughts
Everything you outlined is good. I’ll add my sort of twist on the workflow/principles for how this looks in my head. There is a concrete technique I use for this, so I’ll encode that into the skill/workflow.

In my brain, this skill works in several stages/modes. One is to develop a profile for the leader to use going forward. The second is building and maintaining a rapport map that it can use for all “influence” work. The final mode is to leverage the map toward a specific goal.

Trigger Criteria
Broadly, this should trigger when the user wants to accomplish something when authority lies with someone else. A simple test would be, “Do you need permission to do this?”

Workflow #1 - Rapport Mapping
Trigger - As above
Check -  Are there new players to consider?
If Yes - Conduct a brief interview where we focus on a few questions:
- Name, title, area of responsibility
- What do they respond to/Working style
- Who they have a positive relationship with
- Who they have a negative relationship with
- Your relationship with them (Positive/Negative, shared history, key moments that shape your relationship so far).
These notes are put into a note system that AI has access to.
If No - AI can suggest paths to the needed person, emphasizing positive paths. It can ask if it should include leveraging negative relationships.

Workflow #2 - Influence Building
Trigger - Can be prompted to “resume” from a previous plan or a daily part of work
Check - Has anything new happened (Email, meetings, conversations)? These are to be viewed as new opportunities or pivots to the established map.
Act - AI provides a specific recommendation for a specific “Next person” in the rapport map plan. This recommendation should be informed by broad principles and the individual leader’s comfort (And willingness to stretch). Each touch point must work toward the goal of moving along the rapport map. At some point, someone will have to ask for a seat, introduction, decision, support, etc.
Review - After each interaction, record notes in the note system for the individual or in the goal’s note for AI to leverage and not lose track of.

Interview the Leader
I think it would help to “Prime” the skill with a bit about the individual leader. This is to create an intersection between well-known influence techniques and the leader’s individual preferences, strengths, and weaknesses.

Prime AI: I think the skill should already leverage the principles and material above and be aware of the channels that exist such as getting lunch, DMs, email, meetings, etc.

Interview: I think this can be a light touch, but might benefit from a few interesting questions that probe deeper.
What is your preferred way to interact with peers or superiors?
When is face-to-face better than virtual communication in your experience?
How have you been successful in building rapport in the past?
What feels awkward, clumsy, or unnatural when you’re building rapport with others?
Is building rapport and influence something you’d like to develop? Developing this skill will push you outside of your comfort zone.
Is there influence building material you are familiar with that we can build upon?
Are there sources of influence building I should use and consider when giving recommendations?
What methods or techniques are out of bounds to you?
